What it does

Eleven screens. One question each.

Most portfolio tools are a wall of numbers with no opinion. PerceptFolio is the opposite: every screen exists to answer one decision, and every verdict shows the arithmetic behind it.

Command

One list, every morning: what actually needs you today. Sell signals, breached limits, reviews falling due, idle cash. Most days it says nothing needs you, and that is the correct answer.

Scorecard

Every verdict it has ever issued, marked against the S&P at 30, 90, 180 and 365 days. A BUY is right if it beat the index. A SELL is right if it lagged. You see the record whether or not it flatters the system.

Sizing

Before you trade: what this does to your concentration, your cash buffer, your market exposure, and your own written limits. Being right about the company and wrong about the size is how the money goes.

Drawdown

Not where you might end up — the hole you pass through on the way. Modelled from real market history rather than a bell curve, because crashes cluster and a normal distribution can't represent that.

Supply map

Map who a company depends on, upstream and down. Five holdings that all rely on the same supplier are one bet, not five. No other screen in any tool will show you that.

Your habits

How long you actually hold. Whether the things you sell keep rising without you. Whether you break your own cash rule. Anyone can buy price history; nobody else has yours.

The difference

It writes down what it said, then checks.

Every stock tool on earth will give you a signal. Almost none will tell you how those signals performed, because the honest answer is usually uncomfortable. PerceptFolio logs each verdict the moment it changes, stamps the price and the index alongside it, and marks the result on fixed anniversaries it cannot move.

“You do not get to forecast more until you can prove you forecast well.”

Questions

The ones worth answering.

Is this investment advice?

No, and it is important that you understand why. PerceptFolio applies rules you configure to public data and shows you the arithmetic. When it says BUY, that means "this cleared the bar you set" — not "this will go up". It knows nothing about your circumstances, it has no view on what you should own, and nobody at NorthBridge sees your positions. It is a calculator with an opinion you wrote yourself.

Where does my data go?

Nowhere by default. Your holdings, prices, notes and history live in your own browser's storage. If you turn on sync, it goes to a private storage box on infrastructure you set up and control, protected by a key only you hold. There is no PerceptFolio account database holding your portfolio.

What market data does it use?

Live US quotes and company fundamentals from Finnhub's free tier, and US macro series from the St. Louis Fed. It is honest about the limits: no international coverage, no intraday history, no institutional supply-chain feed. Where a number cannot be sourced for free, the app says so and lets you type it in rather than inventing it.

Why is the scorecard empty when I start?

Because a fixed-horizon track record cannot be faked backwards. To know what a stock did ninety days after a call, something has to look on the day. PerceptFolio marks each call on its own anniversary going forward. That means the first meaningful reading is three months away, and we would rather tell you that than show you a back-test.

Does it place trades?

No, and it never will. There is no broker connection. It tells you what your rules flagged and shows the numbers; you decide, and you execute in your own brokerage.

Who builds this?

NorthBridge, a holding company. PerceptFolio sits in its financial branch and was built as internal infrastructure before it was offered to anyone else. It is still the tool we use ourselves, which is the main reason it refuses to flatter its own output.
