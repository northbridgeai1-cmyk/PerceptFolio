01The terminal

Eleven screens. One question each.

Every screen answers one decision and shows the arithmetic behind it. Nothing is a black box, because a system nobody can audit is a system nobody should act on.

Command

One list each morning: what needs you today. Sell signals, breached limits, stops hit, reviews due, idle cash. Most days it reports that nothing needs you, and that is the correct answer.

Scorecard

Every verdict the system has issued, marked against the index at 30, 90, 180 and 365 days. Reported with a confidence interval, so a thin sample reads as a thin sample rather than a result.

Sizing

Three limits before any trade — policy cap, volatility budget, distance to stop — and the smallest one binds. Being right about the company and wrong about the size is how accounts end.

Drawdown

Not where the book might finish but the hole it passes through. Sampled from real market history rather than a normal curve, because losses arrive in runs and a bell curve cannot represent that.

Supply map

Who a company depends on, upstream and down. Five holdings that all rely on one supplier are one position, not five, and no other screen will surface it.

Behaviour

How long positions are actually held. Whether what gets sold keeps rising without us. Whether the cash rule survives contact with a bad week. The one dataset that cannot be bought.

02The discipline

It writes down what it said, then checks.

Each verdict is logged the moment it changes, stamped with the price and the index, and marked on an anniversary that cannot be moved afterwards. A tool that flatters itself is worse than no tool, because it launders a guess into a number.

“You do not get to forecast more until you can prove you forecast well.”

03Access

Applications are graded, not read.

Anyone can write a paragraph about why they should have access. So the form asks for something that can be checked: three calls, with a target, a stop and a date. In ninety days they will have marked themselves, and the decision is made on evidence rather than on how the paragraph read.

1 Reviewed by a person

Not a queue system and not an autoresponder. Every request is read by someone in the financial branch who can act on it, and you get a straight answer including no.

2 Granted per person

Credentials are issued individually, never to a team, so every action in the ledger traces to someone. Approval specifies whether the account is personal or carries client books.

3 Bring your own data key

The terminal does not resell market data. You connect a free Finnhub key of your own — two minutes, no card. Client work additionally requires the shared macro proxy.

4 Thesis before position

Any position taken through the terminal carries a written thesis, a stop and a deadline recorded when it is opened. A reason invented after the price moves is not a reason.

Live — 18

VaR / ES engine · Historical VaR · Parametric VaR · Expected Shortfall — loss severity from the daily return series. ES is shown as the primary figure because VaR states where the threshold is and nothing about how far past it losses run. That blindness is why Basel moved from VaR to Expected Shortfall.

Volatility forecasting (GARCH 1,1) — fitted by variance targeting, so the model's long-run variance matches the sample rather than implying one the data never showed.

Monte Carlo VaR · Tail risk engine — the drawdown model samples real market history in blocks rather than drawing from a normal curve, because losses cluster and a bell curve cannot represent that.

Portfolio construction — three sizing limits, smallest binds: policy cap, volatility budget, distance to stop.

Correlation and covariance engine · Portfolio risk dashboard · Risk and stress testing — including each holding's share of portfolio variance against its share of the money.

Market regime detection — variance-ratio change detection on index returns.

Momentum strategy · Multi-asset trend following — a four-segment momentum score and a 50/200 EWMA crossover, both graded against the index in parallel.

Stock return and volatility analyser · Regime-aware sizing · End-to-end research pipeline — every verdict logged, marked on fixed anniversaries, reported with a confidence interval.

Impossible on retail data — 16

Not hard. Not expensive. Unavailable at any effort on a free market-data tier.

Derivatives pricing · Volatility surface · Local volatility · Black-Scholes engine · Implied volatility solver · Option Greeks · Binomial pricing · Monte Carlo option pricing — all eight need a live options chain: strikes, expiries, bid and ask. There is none.

Interest rate modelling · Yield curve modelling · XVA — need a term structure and discount curves.

Credit and CDS pricing — needs credit spreads.

Electronic trading · High-frequency LOB model · Microstructure simulator · Optimal execution — need tick-level order book data, and an execution venue this has no connection to and never will.

Alternative data alpha — a budget line, not a feature.

A model built on data you do not have does not fail loudly. It emits a confident number with a Greek letter attached, and you act on it.

Declined on evidence — 11

Mean-variance portfolio optimisation — needs expected returns and is pathologically unstable in them, overweighting whatever holding carries estimation error in its favour. Naive equal weighting has beaten it out of sample in published tests. The stable half — risk contribution, which needs no expected returns — was built instead.

Statistical arbitrage · Pairs trading · Kalman filter pairs · Factor long/short — all four require short positions the data model does not support, executed on two legs by hand, on an edge measured in basis points. Published pairs returns have declined substantially since the approach was publicised.

Moving-average mean reversion — sits in the horizon band where the documented effect runs opposite to the rule's assumption.

Machine learning return predictor — with this sample size it is an overfitting machine that produces its most confident output exactly where it has learned the least.

XVA · FRTB and model validation — regulatory capital machinery. XVA requires counterparties. FRTB satisfies a supervisor. There is no supervisor here.

Factor exposure analyser — proper factor returns are not in the data set; an approximation would be a guess wearing a Greek letter.

Fully systematic trading strategy — there is no broker connection and there will not be one. The terminal reports; a person decides and executes.

The rule that governs this list

No further analytics are added until the call scorecard holds forty marked calls. Evidence arrives at one day per day and no amount of building accelerates it, so every module added before then would change the interface without changing what is known. Most of the list above is risk machinery — it exists to make being wrong survivable, not to make being right more frequent, and treating it as the second thing is the most common error made with it.

05Questions

The ones worth answering.

Is this available to the public?

No. PerceptFolio is internal infrastructure operated by NorthBridge's financial branch. It is not sold, licensed or offered as a service. This page exists so that people who work here have one place to sign in from.

Is any of this investment advice?

No. The terminal applies rules its operator configures to public data and shows the arithmetic. When it says BUY, that means the position cleared the bar that operator set. It has no view on anyone's circumstances, makes no recommendation, and takes no custody. Judgement stays with the person at the keyboard.

Does it promise to beat the market?

It promises nothing about returns, and you will not find a performance figure anywhere in this product. What it does is measure — every call marked against the index on a fixed horizon, reported with a confidence interval wide enough to show when the sample is too thin to mean anything. A tool that tells you it wins is selling. A tool that tells you how often it was wrong is working.

Where does my data live?

In your own browser. Holdings, prices, notes and history are stored locally on the device you use. With sync enabled it goes to a private store controlled by NorthBridge, protected by a key held per device. There is no central database holding anyone's book.

Why is my scorecard empty when I start?

Because a fixed-horizon record cannot be faked backwards. To know what a stock did ninety days after a call, something has to look on the day. The terminal marks each call on its own anniversary going forward, so the first meaningful reading is roughly a quarter out.

Does it place trades?

No, and it never will. There is no broker connection and none is planned. It reports what the rules flagged and shows the numbers; execution happens in your own brokerage, by you.
