Add a Relationship

Supplier and customer links have to be entered by hand. That data is assembled by vendors from filings and transcripts and sold at institutional rates — no free API publishes it. The 10-K is where to find it: any customer worth more than 10% of revenue must be disclosed, usually under "Concentration of Credit Risk" or in the risk factors.

The summary describes the relationship, not the company, so it reads correctly from both ends and is copied to the other company's map automatically. Edit it later on either side and the other side follows, unless you've already written something different there.

What's automatic and what isn't. The centre company's profile and the "similar companies" row come from Finnhub and fill in on their own. Suppliers and customers are yours to enter. Institutional holders were deliberately left out: the free source for that is quarterly 13F filings, which are up to four months stale, exclude short positions entirely, and would list Vanguard, BlackRock and State Street at the top of nearly every company you could look up. A list that says the same thing about everything isn't worth the screen space.

Figures With No Free Feed

CAPE comes from Robert Shiller's spreadsheet and margin debt from FINRA. Neither publishes a free API, so these are typed in. Update them monthly; the cards show how stale they are.

A Buffett figure entered here overrides the one calculated from FRED. Worth doing — FRED's Wilshire series is a price index rather than a dollar market cap, so the calculated version tracks the right direction but isn't the textbook percentage. A number from a source that computes it properly is more trustworthy.

Read these as long-run expectations, not crash warnings. All three describe what returns over the next ten to fifteen years are likely to look like from today's prices. They have almost no power to time the next twelve months. The Buffett indicator has read "expensive" since roughly 2013 and CAPE has been above 30 since 2017, through years of large gains. Use them to decide how much to put in, not when to get out.

Portfolio Risk

Your single-stock scores can't see this: how concentrated you are in one sector, and how correlated your holdings are with each other (owning 5 stocks that all move together isn't 5 bets — it's 1). This uses extra API calls per stock, so it's calculated on demand rather than on every refresh.

Where your risk actually sits

Every holding's share of the portfolio's variance, against its share of the money. A 6% position in a violent name can own 20% of your risk, and no position-size percentage will ever show you that.

Check any stock's risk

Doesn't need to be in your Portfolio or Watchlist — just check the risk/volatility profile of any ticker before you decide whether to add it.

Risk-adjusted return

Return per unit of volatility, from your own daily snapshots. Shown with its confidence interval, because a Sharpe ratio computed on a few months of data carries an error bar usually wider than the number itself — and a point estimate without one is the same false precision as a hit rate without a sample size.

Loss severity — VaR and Expected Shortfall

How bad a bad day gets, measured from your own daily history. These do not predict returns. They describe severity, which is what risk machinery is for — surviving being wrong, not being right more often.

Ranges — what the spread of outcomes looks like

These answer "where might this end up", not "what should I do today". Every one is a distribution under stated assumptions, and every one is wrong in a way the assumptions decide.

Monte Carlo projection

Simulates thousands of random possible futures based on estimated return and volatility, to show a realistic range of outcomes instead of a single guess. The return/volatility inputs below are assumptions you're setting — adjust them to match your own view, they're not guaranteed numbers.

Signals — rules that emit a position today

Not forecasts. A signal reads the prices in front of it and tells you how much to hold right now. It says nothing about where the price is going, and it is separated from the section above for exactly that reason.

Herizen momentum score

Four segments, not four lookbacks. The strategy as described draws lines from one week, two weeks, one month and two months ago to today — but those windows are nested, so the same recent days are counted in all four and a single sharp move pushes every line at once. A +4 built that way is one observation counted four times. This uses non-overlapping segments instead — two months to one month, one month to two weeks, two weeks to one week, one week to today — so a +4 means four separate stretches each rose, which is a genuinely stronger statement.

Read this before you trust it

The published evidence on price trends splits sharply by horizon. Momentum — winners keep winning — is documented at roughly three to twelve months. At one week and one month the documented effect is the opposite: short-term reversal, where recent winners give it back. Three of this rule's four windows sit in that reversal band, which means the honest prior on it is somewhere between "no edge" and "negative edge", before costs.

That is why it is wired in as a position cap and nothing else. It can shrink a position the checklist already cleared. It cannot create a buy, block a sell, or change a verdict. A wrong cap costs you upside; a wrong verdict costs you capital, and this rule has not earned the right to cost you capital.

It is also being graded against two alternatives from today — checklist alone, this alone, and the two combined — on the Market tab. In ninety days that argument settles itself with numbers instead of citations.

This fee % is just a private note, not a billing system. PerceptFolio has no payment processing and never moves money — "Est. fee owed" is a rough math estimate (fee % × unrealized gain, zero on losses), not an invoice. More importantly: taking a cut of a client's investment gains is a performance-based fee, and under SEC Rule 205-3 of the Investment Advisers Act, advisers generally can't charge performance fees except to "qualified clients" who clear specific net-worth/asset thresholds — and that's on top of needing to be a properly registered or exempt adviser in the first place just to manage someone else's investments for pay at all. Recording a number here doesn't make an arrangement compliant. Talk to a securities attorney before you charge anyone a percentage of their gains.

Market Data API (Finnhub — free)

Get a free key at finnhub.io/register (takes ~2 min). Paste it here and it stays in this browser.

Investment Policy

The limits this portfolio is run to. Command checks every holding against these and flags breaches. Set per account, so a client with a three-year horizon can run different limits from your own book — the same stock can be a buy in one portfolio and a hold in another.

How much of the whole portfolio a single position is allowed to put at risk. With a stop set, this is the most you lose if it hits. Without one, it sizes by volatility instead. A flat cap treats a quiet utility and a violent semiconductor as the same bet; this doesn't.

Buy / Sell Rules

Changing a buy or sell bar creates a different system. Every call graded before the change was made by one that no longer exists, so you'll be asked what you expect the change to do and what result would prove it wrong — before you see any data. That's the only defence against searching a small sample until something looks significant.

Rule Change Log

Every change to the buy or sell bars, with the prediction you made at the time. Read it back when a change is a few months old and check whether what you expected is what happened.

Copy Holdings / Watchlist to Another Account

Copies from the account you're in right now into another one on this browser — useful for setting a new client up with a starting book, or cloning your own list into a second account. It copies the positions and tickers only; cash, transaction history, dividends, alerts, and settings are left alone.

Merge adds anything they don't already have and leaves their existing positions untouched. Replace wipes the selected lists on the target account first — that can't be undone, so it asks first.

Sync Across Your Devices

Keeps this account's holdings, watchlist, history and settings the same on your phone and your computer. Paste the same two values on every device. Setup instructions are in SYNC-SETUP.md.

Once connected it syncs automatically — when you open the app, a few seconds after you make changes, and when you close the tab. This is whole-account last-write-wins, not a merge. If you edit on your phone and your laptop without letting them sync in between, the device that saves last overwrites the other. It warns you before clobbering something newer, but the safe habit is to open the app on a device and let it sync before you start editing there.

Backup / Restore

Your data lives in this browser only. Export regularly, and import on a new device to move everything over.
