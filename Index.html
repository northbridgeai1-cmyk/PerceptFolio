What you get

Eleven screens. One question each.

Every screen exists to answer one decision, and every verdict shows the arithmetic behind it. Nothing in here is a black box, because a system nobody can audit is a system nobody should act on.

Command

One list, every morning: what actually needs you today. Sell signals, breached limits, reviews falling due, idle cash. Most days it says nothing needs you, and that is the correct answer.

Scorecard

Every verdict the system has issued, marked against the S&P at 30, 90, 180 and 365 days. A BUY is right if it beat the index; a SELL is right if it lagged. Shown whether or not it flatters the checklist.

Sizing

Before a trade: what it does to concentration, cash buffer, market exposure, and the written position limits. Being right about the company and wrong about the size is how the money goes.

Drawdown

Not where the book might end up — the hole it passes through on the way. Modelled from real market history rather than a bell curve, because crashes cluster and a normal distribution cannot represent that.

Supply map

Map who a company depends on, upstream and down. Five holdings that all rely on the same supplier are one bet, not five. No other screen will surface that.

Behaviour

How long positions are actually held. Whether what gets sold keeps rising without us. Whether the cash rule is kept. The only dataset here that cannot be bought.

The discipline

It writes down what it said, then checks.

The terminal logs each verdict the moment it changes, stamps the price and the index alongside it, and marks the outcome on fixed anniversaries it cannot move. That record is the point of the whole system: an internal tool that flatters itself is worse than no tool, because it launders a guess into a number.

“You do not get to forecast more until you can prove you forecast well.”

Access

How to get in, and what's expected once you are.

Credentials are issued per person by the financial branch. There is no self-service tier and no trial.

1 Request credentials

Email the financial branch with your role and what you'll be using it for. Access is granted per person, never per team, so that every action in the ledger traces to someone.

2 Bring your own data key

The terminal does not resell market data. You connect a free Finnhub key of your own — two minutes, no card. Client work additionally requires the shared macro worker to be configured.

3 Keep client books separate

Each client is a separate profile with its own holdings, rules, thesis notes and policy. Never work a client's book from a personal profile, and never carry positions between them.

4 Write the thesis before the trade

Any position taken through the terminal gets a written thesis and a numeric sell trigger at the time it is opened. A reason invented after the price moves is not a reason.

Questions

The ones worth answering.

Is this available to the public?

No. PerceptFolio is internal infrastructure operated by NorthBridge's financial branch. It is not sold, licensed, or offered as a service, and there is no public sign-up. This page exists so that people who work here have one place to sign in from, not to attract customers.

Is anything here investment advice?

No. The terminal applies rules its operator configures to public data and shows the arithmetic. When it says BUY, that means "this cleared the bar you set" — not "this will go up". It has no view on anyone's circumstances and it does not make recommendations. Judgement stays with the person operating it.

Where does the data live?

In your own browser. Holdings, prices, notes and history are stored locally on the device you are using. If sync is enabled it goes to a private storage instance controlled by NorthBridge, protected by a key held per device. There is no central database holding anyone's book.

Can I use it for my own investing as well as client work?

Yes, as a separate profile. Personal and client books must never share a profile — the terminal enforces separate holdings, rules and policy per account precisely so this line does not blur. Business accounts are blocked from taking positions of their own.

Why is my scorecard empty?

Because a fixed-horizon track record cannot be faked backwards. To know what a stock did ninety days after a call, something has to look on the day. The terminal marks each call on its own anniversary going forward, so the first meaningful reading is roughly three months out.

Does it place trades?

No, and it never will. There is no broker connection and none is planned. It reports what the rules flagged and shows the numbers; execution happens in your brokerage, by you.
