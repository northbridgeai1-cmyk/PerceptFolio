# Prompt for an outside reviewer

Paste everything below the line into another AI, then paste `HANDOFF.md` after it.

Written 2026-09-27 after two reviews came back weak: one recited the handoff's own open-items list
back as recommendations, and the other asked for six things that already exist. Both failed the
same way — they could not see the product, so they reviewed the document and guessed.

---

I want a strategic review of PerceptFolio. Before you answer, four things you must accept, because
the last two reviewers got them wrong and their reviews were worth little as a result.

**1. It is not a cheap Bloomberg. Do not review it as one.**

The line that settles scope arguments: *Bloomberg tells you everything that is happening;
PerceptFolio tells you whether your decisions worked.* It is a research terminal for one person who
buys and sells shares, priced at $760 a month, deliberately not for everyone.

It publicly and permanently refuses: fixed income, options, FX, order execution, chat. It never
places a trade, never quotes a win rate (expectancy with its interval instead), and never edits a
past mark. **Do not recommend any of those.** A review that suggests them has not read this.

**2. You cannot see the terminal. Say so rather than guessing.**

The terminal is behind an access gate. You will see the marketing site and the document I am about
to paste, and nothing else. So:

- Do not tell me to "add" something without saying how you checked it is missing.
- Mark every claim as either **verified from the document** or **assumed**.
- If a recommendation depends on something you could not see, say which.

**3. Do not recommend anything already in the document's own open-items list.**

Section 11 is my list of known gaps. Repeating it back is not a review. If you think one of them is
more urgent than I do, argue that instead — but tell me you are doing so.

**4. What I actually want.**

For each recommendation:

- **What** to add, remove or change.
- **Why**, in terms of the investment workflow — discover, research, decide, monitor, measure.
- **Evidence.** A source for any factual claim about competitors, data providers, pricing or
  licensing. "It would look better" is not a reason. Neither is "Bloomberg has it".
- **Cost of being wrong.** What breaks if I build it and you were mistaken.
- **Verified or assumed**, per point 2.

Rank the list. Put anything that threatens the product commercially (data licensing, redistribution
rights, per-seat terms) above anything that is a feature.

**The question I most want answered, if you only answer one:**

The product's claim is that it tells you whether your decisions worked. That claim needs roughly
138 marked calls across a quarter before the arithmetic says anything at all. A subscriber paying
$760 a month sees "not enough data yet" for months before the product can do the one thing it
promises. What should happen in those months?
